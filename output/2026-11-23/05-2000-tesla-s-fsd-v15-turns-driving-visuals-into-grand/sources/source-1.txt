Tesla has begun rolling out Full Self-Driving (Supervised) v15, calling it a ground-up overhaul of its end-to-end driver assist system. The centerpiece is Reality View, a real-time generative display that swaps out old wireframe graphics for a live, overhead camera angle that lets drivers see and tap to influence what the car plans to do next.
Tesla’s FSD v15 turns driving visuals into Grand Theft Auto
The update swaps blocky wireframes for a photorealistic overhead feed and promises a “25x” safety boost, even as federal regulators continue investigating the software.
The third-person perspective renders buildings, crosswalks, pedestrians, and surrounding traffic with a photorealism that has drawn immediate comparisons to Grand Theft Auto VI (which conveniently launched just days earlier). Even Elon Musk couldn’t resist the comparison on X, posting “FSD is GTA irl” shortly after the update dropped on Saturday.
Under the hood, FSD v15 runs on an upgraded vision encoder shared across Tesla’s FSD, Robotaxi, and Optimus humanoid robot programs. By training the model on millions of online videos, Tesla claims its AI learned to prevent serious driving mistakes 25 times more often while using less computing power. Of course, the update is built for vehicles running Tesla’s newer AI4 (HW4) hardware and upcoming Cybercab platforms, leaving millions of older HW3-equipped cars behind.
Beyond the flashy visuals, v15 packs several practical upgrades. A new Bike Rack Mode lets drivers engage FSD even with hitch accessories attached (though it disables reversing, Summon, and Autopark). The system can now automatically ease off the speed near traffic cameras, with an option to override and permanently mute specific cameras after two cancellations. Tesla also promises sharper hazard avoidance for potholes, standing water, and small animals, alongside smoother speed handling in school zones and bad weather.
Meanwhile, navigation learns preferred neighborhood routes and favorite parking spots over time, and the cabin camera can now track driver attention through hats and sunglasses while dialing back nagging alerts at higher speeds. Tesla has a pretty poor reputation for ensuring its drivers stay alert while using FSD, so hopefully this upgrade can improve that.
Still, Reality View raises an obvious question: is a hyper-detailed, video-game-style feed on the center touchscreen a helpful window into the car’s “brain,” or a tempting new distraction? For drivers who find the live feed overwhelming, Tesla includes a map-only option and a stripped-back “quiet mode”.
Tesla’s bold “25x” safety claim also lands amid intense federal scrutiny. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) is actively investigating roughly 2.9 million FSD-equipped vehicles over reports of Teslas blowing through red lights, crossing railroad tracks improperly, and driving into oncoming lanes. Alongside dozens of cumulative fatalities linked to Tesla’s driver-assistance systems since 2019, the probe is a stark reminder of the gap between Tesla’s autonomous ambitions and a system that still legally requires a human to keep their eyes on the road.
