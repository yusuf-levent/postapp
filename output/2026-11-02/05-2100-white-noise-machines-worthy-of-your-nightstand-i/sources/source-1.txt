I have chronic insomnia. I need a white noise machine, a sleep mask, a pitch-black room, and maybe some light tranquilizers just to fall asleep. (I'm joking, sort of.) If I'm traveling and forget my sound machine, I have to play white noise from my perpetually dying phone. These devices are simple, even as companies keep adding bells and whistles. They now come in all shapes and sizes, with options for playing different noise frequencies and soothing nature sounds—whether you need one at home or a portable model for life on the go. And they’re not just for kids or babies; if, like me, you want to quiet your inner monologue and finally drift off, this guide is for you.
We've tested everything: the best sleep trackers, sunrise alarm clocks, mattresses, and extreme alarm clocks. Check out all of the WIRED Reviews team's sleep coverage for our recommendations.
Note: If you're buying for a child, keep sound machines to no more than 50 decibels and farther than 200 centimeters (6.5 feet) from where your baby sleeps.
A Note on “White Noise”
“Colors” of noise are measured similarly to light. Pure white noise is a mix of all the frequencies humans can hear, so while these machines are often called “white noise" machines, white noise itself isn't always a relaxing sound (think of the static on your TV). Pink and brown noise are at the lower end of the spectrum, aren't as grating, and have been found to help people sleep a little more deeply. If you've seen pink noise, brown noise, and green noise amid all the white noise machine chatter, it's because, more often than not, you'll find those options bundled into one machine. If you think of sound waves as being loosely analogous to light waves, then the different colors of noise refer to different parts of the sound spectrum. The fun thing about using a sound machine for sleep is that you get to experiment with these noise frequencies to find what works best for you.

Can White Noise Machines Typically Run All Night?
If you plan on keeping your white noise machine playing sound on a loop all night, make sure first that it has the capacity to do so. Some machines run on 30-, 60-, or 90-minute timers that auto-shut off, while others are continuous.
Does a Fan Make a Good White Noise Machine?
In a pinch, a desk or box fan can stand in for a white noise machine. It provides steady noise to help you fall asleep and can also keep hot sleepers comfortable. But if you’d rather not cool the room or want a wider range of sounds, a sound machine is the better choice.
