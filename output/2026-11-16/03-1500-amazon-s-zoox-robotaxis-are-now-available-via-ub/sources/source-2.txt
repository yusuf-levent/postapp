Zoox robotaxis are officially available through the Uber app in Las Vegas, marking the Amazon-owned autonomous vehicle company’s first partnership with a third-party ride-hailing platform.
The launch on Monday is a milestone for both companies. Uber gets to add another partner to its app as part of its broader strategy to become the primary purveyor of autonomous vehicle technology in North America, Europe, and the Middle East. Uber has partnered with more than 30 companies developing autonomous vehicle technology for sidewalk delivery robots, self-driving cars, robotaxis, and drones — though not all of those have turned into commercial deployments yet.
For Zoox, the partnership and launch gives it access to more potential customers who might otherwise not try its robotaxi, a needed boost as it trails market leaders Tesla and Waymo.
The partnership is expected to extend beyond Las Vegas. Zoox plans to launch a commercial robotaxi service in Los Angeles this year, and make rides available via the Uber app in 2027. For now, all eyes are on Las Vegas, Zoox’s first commercial market and a city where Uber has ambitious robotaxi plans.
The two companies also announced a multi-year partnership with MGM Resorts International: they’re rideshare and robotaxi partners for MGM Resorts properties in Las Vegas. Uber and Zoox said they’ll work with MGM Resorts to improve pickup and drop-off locations, along with signage to help riders navigate sprawling MGM properties and find the robotaxis.
Earlier this year, the Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved a permit that allowed Uber to deploy up to 1,000 robotaxis in Clark County — home to Las Vegas — through its partnerships with Hyundai subsidiary Motional and Zoox.
Zoox’s addition to the Uber app comes with some initial limitations. Customers won’t be able to select a Zoox robotaxi — at least initially. Instead, people who request an UberX, Uber Comfort, Uber Electric, Uber Comfort Electric, or Wait & Save ride may be matched with one of Zoox’s custom-built robotaxis once they hail a ride. Customers can increase their chances of getting a Zoox robotaxi by opting for autonomous rides through “ride preferences” in the Uber app’s settings.
Uber plans to introduce a dedicated autonomous option in its app as “availability and reliability improve,” Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said during the company’s third-quarter earnings call in November.
“Las Vegas will become the first U.S. city where riders can access two fully driverless partners through the Uber app,” he said on the call. “We will begin with limited fleets and expand supply over the coming months, with plans to introduce a dedicated autonomous option in our app.”
The plan to offer a dedicated autonomous option marks a shift in Uber’s approach to robotaxis, including its partnership with Waymo.
That exclusive partnership, which matches Waymo robotaxis with Uber riders in Austin and Atlanta, will end in January 2028. Waymo plans to offer its rides directly through its own smartphone app as it does in other cities. TechCrunch previously confirmed with Uber that it still plans to host Waymo robotaxis on its app for as long as the two companies agree.
And unlike its partnership with other AV developers, Uber told TechCrunch that it will not provide maintenance and other services for Zoox in the Las Vegas market.
