The UK pulled down its online pensions services due to a 'cyber incident'
Experts believe the department was targeted by hackers.
The UK has taken down some of the Department for Work and Pensions' online services after identifying a "cyber incident" affecting its systems. According to the BBC, the department's portals went down late on Thursday after it detected "unusual activity." It remained inaccessible, as of Saturday morning in the UK. The pensions department said it's working with the National Cyber Security Centre and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate the incident and bring the services back online. It will likely take some time before they release the details of their investigation, but experts believe the department was targeted by hackers.
David Knott, the UK's former chief technology office, told the news organization: "[The government has] taken down some of the citizen-facing services, which implies to me that there's some kind of attack could be mounted through the front door." John Curry, a cybersecurity expert from Bath Spa University, said he thinks the government shut down the pensions portals to prevent data from being stolen by the bad actors.
While it's still unclear what happened, and we've yet to hear whether AI is involved, this incident has raised concerns about cybersecurity as a whole. Governments and companies around the world will have to make investments and find ways to keep their systems protected from hackers who use advanced AI technologies to aid them.
The UK government says that despite the incident, "regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal." Still, it's asking people to be more vigilant and to keep an eye out for phishing attempts.
